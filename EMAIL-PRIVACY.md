## Proton Mail — https://proton.me/mail — Hacked: Not Hacked

• Zero-Access Integrity: The platform uses client-side encryption, ensuring that even under infrastructure attacks or server seizures, stored inbox data is mathematically unreadable by Proton or third parties.

• Legal Disclosures: Under Swiss court orders, Proton cannot decrypt communications but is legally compelled to log and hand over account metadata (such as IP addresses and account creation times) to law enforcement agencies.

Authoritative Source: Proton Mail Official Threat 
DDoS attack: 2015-11-03; availability attack, not a confirmed data breach.

## Mailfence — https://mailfence.com/ — Hacked: Not Hacked.
The service has never suffered a centralized database breach or infrastructure compromise.
However, you should keep these two key facts in mind:
• Plaintext Storage: Mailfence stores your emails in plaintext on their servers unless you manually set up and use OpenPGP encryption.
• User-Level Risks: Any individual account compromises are caused by phishing attacks or password reuse from other leaked websites, not an exploit of Mailfence itself.
